description: "The path of a request, from the signed GNAP grant to the credential in the holder's wallet."
---

<Issuance />

## 1. Ask

The participant sends a GNAP grant to `POST /api/v1/gate/access`, naming the credential it wants. The address is in the DID document of the authority, as its `CredentialIssuer` service.

The request must be signed with **HTTP Message Signatures**, using the key in `client.key`: a JWK or a PEM certificate. Heimdall verifies the signature before anything else, and rejects credentials its role cannot issue.

## 2. Prove

What happens next depends on the `interact` block of the grant:

| The grant asks for | Response | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `oid4vp` in `interact.start` | A presentation request | Credentials that depend on others |
| Anything else | The credential offer, straight away | The certificate path |

**With a certificate**, the authority reads what it needs from the X.509 certificate. Legal credentials take their value from the `organizationIdentifier` attribute, as [Identity](/docs/identity#legal-credentials) explains.

**With a presentation**, the wallet fetches the definition at `GET /api/v1/verifier/pd/{state}` and sends its presentation to `POST /api/v1/verifier/verify/{state}`. The authority asks for the credentials in `verify_req_config.vcs_requested`, and keeps those it verifies to build the new one.

## 3. Decide

The decision is automatic. On the certificate path the offer already came in the first response, so the participant goes straight to the next step.

On the presentation path the request is approved once the presentation is verified. The interaction finishes and the participant continues the grant at `POST /api/v1/gate/continue/{id}` with its `interact_ref`, signing that call as well. The response brings the credential offer.

Every request appears in the console and in `GET /api/v1/approver/all`.

## 4. Redeem

The offer goes to the wallet of the participant, which completes OID4VCI with the pre-authorized code flow:

1. `POST /api/v1/issuer/token` exchanges the code for an access token.
2. `POST /api/v1/issuer/credential` sends a proof of possession of the holder key and receives the credential, signed by the authority.

The participant then appears in `GET /api/v1/minions/all` and in the federated catalog.
